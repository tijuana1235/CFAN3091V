Week 1 Literature Intake

Question, Claim, and Evidence

Download the two-page Literature Intake Form for printing. Print it double-sided if you prefer one sheet.

NoteFirst Pass, Not Final Answer

We will complete this intake together. You are not expected to arrive with final answers, and you may revise any entry as our reading changes. Keep the completed form. It is the beginning of your claims-and-evidence record, not an assignment submission.

Paper

Davis, A. S. (2010). Cover-crop roller-crimper contributes to weed management in no-till soybean. Weed Science, 58(3), 300-309. Open the article record using its DOI.

Retrieve the paper through the University Libraries or another lawful source. The course does not redistribute the publisher PDF.

1. Topic

In a short phrase, what broad subject is this paper about?

2. Research Question

What are the authors trying to find out? Record the page, paragraph, or passage that helped you locate the question.

3. Central Claim

Record one consequential conclusion or interpretation. Keep its scope and level of certainty visible. Record its location in the paper.

4. Supporting Evidence

What specific result, observation, estimate, figure, table, quotation, theme, case, model, passage, or other evidence is connected to that claim? Name the evidence type and its exact location.

5. Connection

How does the evidence support the claim? State the reasoning that connects them. Do not write only “it proves it.”

6. Boundary

What does this evidence not establish? Name one limit involving scope, comparison, measurement, context, uncertainty, or interpretation.

7. Uncertainty

What would you inspect next before treating this entry as settled?

First Claims-and-Evidence Ledger Entry

Use the work above to create a compact entry you can revisit later. Record:

  1. the claim;
  2. the evidence and its location;
  3. why the evidence is relevant to this claim;
  4. a limit or open question; and
  5. the current status: tentative, needs closer reading, or revised.

The Reading Protocol provides the longer procedure we will reuse later in the semester.
