Week 5 Question Workshop

Worksheets for Both Meetings

Week 5 uses two printable worksheets, one per meeting. Neither is a milestone on its own. Monday’s sorting sheet is in-class work you keep, and Wednesday’s rebuilt question goes into Progress Report 2, due Wednesday, October 7.

Copies are provided in class. Print your own if you prefer to work on a sheet you have already read.

Monday: Match the Question to the Approach

Download the printable question sort (PDF)

Twelve unlabeled research questions, written for this exercise rather than taken from anyone’s thesis. Working in pairs, you will:

  • decide what form the answer to each question would have to take;
  • name the approach each question requires, and which ones could go either way;
  • record the two questions you disagreed about most, and why;
  • rewrite one question so it requires the other approach; and
  • name one conclusion the rewritten version supports that the original did not.

The last two steps are the ones that teach. A question is not quantitative or qualitative because of its topic, but because of what would count as an answer.

Wednesday: Rebuild Your Question

Download the printable question rebuild worksheet (PDF)

This worksheet walks your own question through a slot structure, a rewrite, the three standards, and a partner test. It offers two structures and asks you to pick one:

  • Structure A for comparison questions, using population, intervention or exposure, comparison, and outcome.
  • Structure B for descriptive, exploratory, and process questions, using population or cases, phenomenon of interest, context, and evidence.

Most thesis questions in this college are not comparisons, so Structure B is the common case rather than the fallback.

Leave a slot empty if it is empty. The worksheet is built so an empty slot stays visible, because that is usually where the design problem is.

The partner test

The second half of the worksheet is the part to protect time for. Your partner asks what you would actually do to answer the question, and what in your proposal would change if the answer came back opposite to what you expect.

If nothing would change, the worksheet asks you to decide which of two things is true: the question is not really open, or the proposal is not really resting on it. Both are worth finding out in October rather than in November.

Submission and Access

Keep both worksheets. Neither is handed in during Week 5. The rebuilt question carries into Progress Report 2 and the Search Record, which is uploaded to Canvas on Wednesday, October 7.

If an access need makes the printed worksheets unworkable, contact the instructor. The same prompts can be supplied in an accessible digital format without changing the work or the deadline.
