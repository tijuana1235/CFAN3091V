Assignments Overview

CFAN 3091V | What You Will Produce

How Grading Works

CFAN 3091V uses an A-F grading basis. The final assignment weights and grade calculation are determined through specifications-grading bundles rather than percentage weights. See the syllabus grading section for the A-F bundle requirements and resubmission policy.

Submission Method by Week

Every graded milestone has a Canvas gradebook entry. The gradebook is where you see the result; it is not an instruction to upload every assignment. Use the normal submission method below.

For paper work, use the format stated on the assignment page. For the Week 1 freewrite, that means Times New Roman, 12 point, double spaced, one-inch margins, continuous line numbers, and single-sided printing. If an absence or access need makes a paper hand-in unworkable, contact the instructor for a digital alternative. See the syllabus submission policy.

Milestone Tracker

There are 17 milestones across the 15 weeks. Weeks 2 and 12 each carry two, because their parts are handed in or submitted on different days; every other week carries one. This list is the grading scale: the bundles in the syllabus are counted against it, so 80 percent means 14 of 17 and 60 percent means 11 of 17. Use the tracker for your course track. Track placement is confirmed with the instructor in Week 1.

Assessed Separately

The process portfolio is named separately in each grading bundle and does not count among the 17 milestones. Neither does the Course-Track Survey, which is a component of Milestone 1.

Essential Document Milestones

Milestones 12a, 12b, 13, and 15 are required for every passing grade. They establish that you produced a complete document, sent the released version for professional review, discussed it, and submitted the revised final version. Milestone 12b is separately essential because uploading an advisor-ready draft and never sending it is not a completed release. The milestone count allows recovery from missed supporting work; it does not replace the course’s culminating document.

Major Deliverables

Full Thesis Proposal (Course Assignment)

This is the substantial document you develop and submit for CFAN 3091V. It is not the same thing as the shorter UHP Thesis Proposal Form. The complete proposal includes the sections required by your program. At minimum, the working course structure includes:

  • title and project identification;
  • introduction and research question;
  • literature review;
  • proposed methods;
  • expected results and alternative outcomes;
  • discussion of significance and limitations;
  • figures or tables when appropriate; and
  • references.

Program-specific requirements take precedence over this working list.

Final Thesis Draft (Fall Graduation Track)

Students graduating in Fall 2026 submit a complete thesis rather than a new proposal. The thesis must reflect the Primary Advisor and Proposal Committee review, meet applicable program requirements, and be filed with the UHP Thesis Completion Form by December 14, 2026. On December 16, students submit the process portfolio and the completion-submission record to the course. Committee approval remains an institutional decision, not a course grade assigned by the instructor.

UHP Thesis Proposal Form (University Requirement)

This is a separate, shorter administrative submission through the Honors Reporting Center. It asks for your thesis title, a brief prospectus, and your committee chair. For spring or summer graduates, the current PDF lists a December 1 deadline; fall graduates have a June 30 deadline. Confirm the current date and any earlier department deadline with your advisor. The Week 13 instructor conversation does not replace this filing.

Foundations Milestones

The Weeks 1-4 milestones move from the course-track survey and an interest and direction freewrite through topic narrowing and faculty identification to a primary-advisor plan. See Foundations Milestones, which also covers what to submit if no faculty member has agreed by Sep 30.

Progress Reports

Short structured accounts of where the project stands. Two of them serve as your individual-meeting agenda. See the Progress Reports page for the template, the standard, and the Professional Review Plan.

Written Assignments

Four milestones contain the course’s main writing assignments: the Week 2 writing exercise and topic funnel, the Method and Feasibility Blueprint, the Literature Synthesis Draft, and the Introduction, Literature Review, and Methods Draft. Milestone 2b is the separate Requirements and Outreach participation worksheet. The writing assignments escalate, and each produces material the next one uses. See the Written Assignments page.

The Document

Your proposal or thesis passes three checkpoints: advisor-ready and released after the Week 12 screen, discussed with the instructor and confirmed in professional review in Week 13, and final in Week 15. The Fall Graduation thesis is due Dec 14; the Proposal Development document is due Dec 16. See The Document.

Process Portfolio

The portfolio documents development across searching, reading, writing, feedback, revision, mentor communication, and proposal or thesis decisions. See the Process Portfolio page.

Instructor Conversation and Release Checkpoint

The Week 13 conversation asks you to explain the proposal or thesis, respond to questions about evidence and design, and identify the revisions required before the document is sent to the Primary Advisor and/or Proposal Committee.

Peer Reviews

Two structured student peer reviews focus on the reader’s experience, the evidence in the draft, and the writer’s stated goals. See the Peer Review Guidelines.

Professional Review

After the Week 12 release screen, send the revised document to the Primary Advisor and/or Proposal Committee. Week 13 confirms that the document was sent and uses the instructor conversation to sharpen the review questions. Submit evidence of professional review, a response memo, and a prioritized revision plan in Week 14. You are assessed on preparation, professional communication, and your use of feedback, not on how quickly another person responds.

If no substantive response has arrived by Dec 9, a documented send, a follow-up message, a self-review against the reviewer criteria, and a prioritized revision plan satisfy the milestone in full. A dated, redacted record and your own summary are sufficient; raw email threads and private reviewer information are not required. See Week 14 for what to submit. Tell the instructor during the week rather than on Dec 9.
