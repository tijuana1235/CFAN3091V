The Document

Advisor-Ready, Released, and Final

Three Versions, Three Standards

The document you submit in Week 15 is the third version of one thing, not a document written at the end of the term. It passes three checkpoints, and each has a different standard.

The release screen in Week 12 protects two things: your reviewers’ time, and your standing with them. A committee member who receives an incomplete draft remembers it. Nothing goes out until the document has passed the published readiness criteria and the instructor has cleared it for release.

What the Document Contains

The complete document includes the sections required by your program. The working course structure is:

  • title and project identification;
  • introduction and research question;
  • literature review;
  • proposed methods;
  • expected results and alternative outcomes, or actual results and discussion for Fall Graduation students;
  • discussion of significance and limitations;
  • figures or tables when appropriate; and
  • references.

Program-specific requirements take precedence over this working list. This is also not the UHP Thesis Proposal Form, which is a separate administrative filing asking only for a title, brief prospectus, and committee chair.

Advisor-Ready, Week 12

This is the term most likely to be misread, so here is the standard plainly.

Advisor-ready does not mean finished, polished, or free of problems. It means a reader who is not you can follow the argument from question to method without you sitting beside them explaining what a section was supposed to say.

A draft is advisor-ready when:

  • every required section exists in some form, with no placeholder headings standing in for unwritten content;
  • the research question appears in the introduction, stated directly;
  • the literature review is organized around themes and supports the specific opening your question addresses;
  • the methods would let a reader judge whether the question can actually be answered;
  • figures, tables, and captions appropriate to the project are present, and each caption stands on its own;
  • the abstract is drafted; and
  • references are complete and Zotero-generated.

The Monday assembly and whole-document exchange test exactly this: whether the document reads without the writer explaining the missing connections. Do not send the advisor-ready version until the Week 12 release screen is complete.

Also bring a short conversation brief naming the document’s central argument, its strongest section, its most serious remaining problem, and your questions for me. Naming your own worst problem is not a confession. It is the fastest way to spend the twenty minutes well.

Whole-Document Review and Revision Memo

Before the release screen, exchange the complete draft with a peer. This is an integration check, not a third graded peer review. The reader traces one chain through the whole document:

  1. What is the research question and why does it matter?
  2. What does the literature establish, complicate, or leave unresolved?
  3. How does the proposed method answer that exact question?
  4. What outcomes would be distinguishable using that method?
  5. What limits, feasibility constraints, or program requirements remain?

The writer submits a one-page revision memo naming the three most important changes made after that review, the evidence in the draft that prompted each change, and any issue that still requires expert judgment.

Week 12 Release Screen

Upload the advisor-ready document and required release materials to Canvas by Monday, Nov 23. The instructor uses the core rubric below for a bounded readiness screen during Week 12. Complete the required release revisions and send the document to the Primary Advisor and/or Proposal Committee no later than Wednesday, Nov 25. The Week 13 conversation continues the critique while the professional review is underway.

Released, Week 12

After the release screen, make the required revisions, then send the document using the Professional Review Plan you confirmed in Week 11.

The release revisions are deliberately scoped. They are the changes that would otherwise make a reviewer misunderstand the project, not every improvement the document could use. Chasing the second list is how students miss the review window.

The Week 13 milestone requires the instructor conversation and confirmation that the released document reached the professional reviewers. See how the conversation is counted for the difference between the milestone and the conversation rating.

Core Proposal and Thesis Rubric

Program-specific requirements may add criteria. They do not replace these course-level standards.

Every dimension must be satisfactory for milestone 15. A dimension rated not yet satisfactory is returned with the specific missing evidence and may be revised under the course resubmission policy when the institutional deadline allows it.

Final, Week 15

The final version incorporates your response to professional review. What changed, and why, is documented in the Week 14 response memo and revision plan.

The Week 15 readiness check is the last pass before applying the rubric:

Proposal Development students upload the final course document to Canvas by Wednesday, December 16, with the process portfolio. Fall Graduation students submit the final thesis through the UHP completion process by Monday, December 14, then submit the process portfolio and completion-submission record to Canvas on December 16.

NoteFall Graduation Track

The three checkpoints work the same way, on a complete thesis rather than a proposal. Your Week 12 submission is the Advisor-ready Full Thesis Draft. Your final thesis, incorporating the Primary Advisor and Proposal Committee review, must be filed with the UHP Thesis Completion Form by Monday, Dec 14. Committee review and approval are due Monday, Dec 21. On Wednesday, Dec 16, upload the process portfolio and completion-submission record to Canvas. A course submission does not file the UHP form automatically.

TipAfter This Course

Keep treating the document as a living plan rather than a promise. Revise it when new evidence, feasibility constraints, or advisor feedback changes what the project can support.
