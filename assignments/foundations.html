Foundations Milestones

Weeks 1 through 4

What This Phase Is For

The first four milestones do one job: put a project and a person in place. By the end of Week 4 you should have a domain narrow enough to ask a question about, and a faculty member who has agreed to advise you. Everything after Week 4 assumes both.

Milestones 1, 2, and 4 are specified below. Milestone 3 uses the recurring Progress Report format.

Milestone 1: Course-Track Survey and Freewrite

Milestone 1 has two participation components because Labor Day leaves Week 1 with a single meeting:

  1. Submit the Course-Track Survey in Canvas by Friday, September 11. Complete it before class on Wednesday, September 9 if you can; it helps confirm which course track fits you. The survey earns participation credit when submitted.
  2. Bring the Interest, Motivation, and Direction Freewrite to class on Monday, September 14, use it during the topic-funnel activity, and hand it in at the end of class.

Milestone 1 is complete when the survey has been submitted and the Freewrite meets the exploratory standard below. The survey is not a sixteenth milestone.

Open the accessible assignment sheet or download the printable PDF.

In-Class Davis Paper Intake

The paper intake is guided practice completed together during class. It is not submitted or graded. Keep the intake and your marked-up article as the first artifacts in your claims-and-evidence record.

  • Open the accessible Week 1 Literature Intake Form, which includes the two-page PDF for printing. Bring a printed copy if you prefer to have your own; copies will also be available in class.
  • Retrieve Davis (2010) through the University Libraries or another lawful source using the article DOI. Bring the paper in a format you can annotate.
  • Use the Reading Protocol as a starting procedure for locating the question, claim, evidence, and boundary.
  • See Week 1: Taking a Paper Apart Together for the class sequence and the distinction among these parts.

The form asks you to record candidate answers rather than an answer key. We will point to passages, compare interpretations, and revise the entry together.

Freewrite: What to Submit

Do not begin with a faculty directory. Begin by writing toward something you care enough to investigate.

Write informally about:

  • problems, systems, questions, or experiences that hold your attention;
  • why those interests matter to you;
  • what motivates you to investigate or work on them;
  • the kinds of activities or work you enjoy doing;
  • a role, job, profession, or career that might involve that kind of work;
  • what you already know and what you would need to explore; and
  • one uncertainty you want help thinking through.

This is exploratory writing. It does not need a settled thesis topic, a research question, faculty names, or polished prose. Its purpose is to give you material for the Week 2 topic-funnel exercise.

Format the freewrite as 1–2 pages in Times New Roman, 12 point, double spaced, with one-inch margins and continuous line numbers. Print it single sided. Bring it to class Monday, use it during the topic-funnel activity, and hand it in at the end of class. The result will appear in Canvas, but there is no routine Canvas upload. The literature intake form stays with you and is not part of the submission.

Satisfactory Standard

The freewrite is satisfactory when it:

  • responds specifically to the prompts rather than listing topics alone;
  • identifies at least one meaningful interest or motivation;
  • connects that interest to a possible kind of work, role, or future direction; and
  • names something you still need to explore or one uncertainty you want help thinking through.

Grammar, certainty, and having a settled topic are not evaluated.

The survey component is satisfactory when it is submitted. Survey answers are used for course planning, not evaluated as right or wrong.

NoteFall Graduation Track

Use the same freewrite through the thesis you are already completing. Address why the project mattered, how it connects to work or a future direction, what still motivates you, what you have learned about the work you want to do, and the most important uncertainty or threat to completion.

The same satisfactory standard applies. The formal thesis-status, committee, and deadline audit occurs in the planning sequence that follows.

Milestone 2: Week 2 Foundations Workshops

Milestone 2 has work from both Week 2 meetings. Complete both printable worksheets in class and hand them in on paper.

The Wednesday component is an in-class participation check based on the work submitted. Attendance alone is not sufficient. The combined Milestone 2 result appears in Canvas after both worksheets are reviewed; there is no routine Canvas upload.

Milestone 2 is satisfactory when both worksheets meet the standards published on their assignment pages. A rough thesis direction is acceptable. Missing sections, generic candidate names without a work-specific connection, or an email draft with no concrete request are not yet satisfactory.

NoteFall Graduation Track

Complete the Monday writing diagnostic and topic funnel through the thesis you are finishing. On Wednesday, use the track prompts to audit completion requirements, connect unresolved decisions to the appropriate Primary Advisor or Proposal Committee member, and draft a specific action or review-window message.

Milestone 4: Primary Advisor Identification

Week 4, due Wednesday, September 30, on paper.

Use the printable Primary Advisor Identification form or Fall Graduation Literature and Reference Audit. The same page also provides the Search Workbook used during both Week 4 meetings.

This milestone asks you to name the faculty member who has agreed to serve as your Primary Advisor. The work leading to it is spread across three weeks: the Week 1 freewrite feeds the Week 2 topic funnel, you identify candidates and draft and peer-test one message in class, then complete the other two by Friday, September 18. Send all three messages no later than Monday, September 21.

That sequencing is deliberate. Sending happens early enough to preserve the Week 4 follow-up window. Bring copies of the sent messages and any replies to your Week 3 meeting so we can decide the next action.

What You Are Asking Someone To Do

Faculty say yes more readily when they know the size of the commitment. It is roughly seven touchpoints across the term, the largest being one 30-minute meeting in Weeks 2 to 4 and a document review around Dec 3 to 9. The full list is on the Advisor FAQ, which also contains a template letter you may send them.

Point them at that page. A request that comes with a clear scope is easier to accept than one that does not.

Satisfactory

The milestone is satisfied by naming a faculty member who has agreed, with:

  • their name, department, and the date they agreed;
  • how the agreement was made, meaning a meeting, an email reply, or a conversation you can point to; and
  • confirmation that they know the approximate December review window.

If No One Has Agreed by September 30

This is the one milestone that depends on whether another person replies to email, and a silent inbox is not a failure of your work. It is assessed on your professional communication, which is entirely within your control.

If no faculty member has agreed by Sep 30, hand in instead:

  • the three messages you sent, with the dates you sent them;
  • at least one follow-up, sent no sooner than five business days after the original and no later than Sep 28;
  • any replies received, including declines; and
  • a short note on what you will try next, naming at least one further candidate.

That submission is satisfactory. Then come see me. A stalled advisor search is usually a targeting problem or a timing problem, and both are fixable, but only if I know about it in September rather than October.

ImportantDo Not Wait Until September 30 To Tell Me

If all three of your candidates have declined by your Week 3 meeting, say so there. I can suggest faculty, and an introduction that comes from me lands differently than a cold email. The students who struggle here are not the ones whose first choices said no. They are the ones who waited.

NoteFall Graduation Track

Your Primary Advisor is already in place, so Week 4 asks for something else: a Literature and Reference Audit identifying missing sources, incomplete citations, unsupported background claims, and reference-library problems that must be resolved before the full thesis review.

Satisfactory means the audit names specific gaps with the sections they affect, rather than a general statement that the references need work.

Resubmission

Any milestone rated not yet satisfactory may be revised and resubmitted once within one week. See the resubmission policy.
