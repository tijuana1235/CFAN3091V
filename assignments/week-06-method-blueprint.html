Week 6 Method and Feasibility Blueprint

Milestone 6 Forms for Both Tracks

Milestone 6 is a design memo, due on paper Wednesday, October 14. It carries the question work of Week 5 and the evidence work of Week 6 into a form your advisor can review.

It is a memo, not a fill-in-the-blank worksheet. The printable forms below give you the headings and the prompts under each. Write in whatever form makes the decision reviewable, add a table where one helps, and type it instead if you prefer, using the same headings. Length is not the target.

Where an item genuinely does not fit your field, write not applicable and say why. Do not invent details to fill a box.

Proposal Development Track

Download the printable Method and Feasibility Blueprint (PDF)

Eight sections:

  1. the research question, the design it requires, and what a single strand cannot deliver for it;
  2. the unit, population, system, cases, or materials you will study;
  3. the evidence or data you need and how you expect to obtain it;
  4. the collection, measurement, comparison, and analysis steps you can name now;
  5. feasibility constraints, including ethics, permissions, and human-subjects review where they apply, and which constraint is largest;
  6. one important risk and a feasible alternative;
  7. one published claim narrowed to what its evidence supports; and
  8. one specific design question for your Primary Advisor.

Satisfactory

A reader can trace the question to the proposed evidence and analysis, identify the largest feasibility constraint, and see what still requires advisor or specialist judgment. The claim example must show both the original wording and the narrowed version you would accept. Section one must name what one strand cannot deliver on its own; if you mark the design mixed, it must also name the connecting logic and the point where the two strands meet.

Section four is the one students most often skip past. Naming what you have not yet decided is worth more than a confident guess, because an unresolved decision your advisor can see is a decision they can help with.

Fall Graduation Thesis Completion Track

Download the printable Thesis Method, Analysis, and Feasibility Audit (PDF)

The same structure, aimed at work already done:

  1. the design actually used, where it departed from the plan, and, where more than one strand is involved, the point at which they meet;
  2. the data or evidence obtained;
  3. analysis complete and outstanding;
  4. ethics or permissions status, when applicable;
  5. the strongest claim your completed work supports;
  6. at least one claim needing qualification, in both its current and narrowed wording;
  7. the largest completion risk and a dated response to it; and
  8. one specific question for your Primary Advisor or Proposal Committee.

Satisfactory

A reader can see what the completed work supports, what it does not, the largest completion risk, and a dated response to that risk. Where the work uses more than one strand, the audit must say what each one carries and where they meet.

Submission and Access

Hand in the form for your track on paper at the end of Wednesday’s class. The result appears in Canvas; do not upload it. Feedback is brief and aimed at your next draft and your advisor check, which is the tier described on the Written Assignments page.

If an absence or access need makes the paper hand-in unworkable, contact the instructor for an arranged digital alternative.
