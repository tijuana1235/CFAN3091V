Week 1: Taking a Paper Apart Together

Phase 1: Foundations

Learning Objectives

By the end of our one meeting this week, you should be able to:

  • describe the purpose of the course, identify your likely course track, and name the major professional-review and completion dates;
  • explain why a paper’s topic, research question, central claim, and supporting evidence are different things;
  • follow an instructor-led paper dissection and record a first, tentative claim-and-evidence entry; and
  • begin exploring the interests, motivations, and possible directions that will feed next week’s topic-funnel work.

You are not expected to dissect a research article independently after one class. This first pass gives us shared language and a procedure we will reuse and strengthen throughout the semester.

One Meeting This Week

Labor Day falls on Monday, September 7, and instruction begins Tuesday, September 8. Our first and only meeting this week is Wednesday, September 9. We have 75 minutes. We will meet one another, make the course and University Honors Program (UHP) timelines visible, and take one research paper apart together.

Before Our First Meeting

  • Complete the course-track survey in Canvas. If you are unsure which track fits, come to class and we will sort it out together.
  • Locate the Davis (2010) article listed below through the University Libraries or another lawful source. Bring a copy in a format you can annotate. If printing or annotating the article creates an access barrier, contact me before class so we can make a workable plan.
  • Bring paper and something to write with. I will bring the paper-dissection handout.

Part 1: Introductions

You will receive a table nameplate when you arrive. Put the name you want me and your classmates to use on the front. You may add pronouns if you want to.

The back is for me rather than for public sharing. It asks for:

  1. your full name as it appears in Canvas;
  2. how to pronounce your name;
  3. where you are from or consider home;
  4. your degree path or program;
  5. your year in school;
  6. whether you are graduating in Fall 2026;
  7. two favorite colors; and
  8. one unusual, interesting, or “weird” fact you would like me to know.

You may skip any personal prompt. These cards stay with me and are not graded.

For the brief spoken introduction, share your preferred name, program or degree path, and one research interest, motivating problem, or optional unusual fact. The introductions take time, and that time matters. This course depends on conversation, individual meetings, and feedback across disciplines. We begin by learning who is in the room.

Part 2: How This Course Runs

We work mainly at the board and on paper. We will make questions, claims, evidence, false starts, and revisions visible while we work. The course site provides the schedule, assignment criteria, readings, and course materials you need outside the room. Canvas is reserved for assignment submission, grades, private feedback, and student records such as the course-track survey.

Most students follow the Proposal Development Track. Students graduating in Fall 2026 who have already filed the UHP Thesis Proposal Form and are actively completing the thesis follow the Fall Graduation Thesis Completion Track. We confirm your track this week.

During our syllabus tour, we will focus on the course workflow, the two tracks, the milestone and revision system, individual meetings, and the dates that shape the semester. One date needs to be visible from the beginning: the released complete proposal or thesis goes to the Primary Advisor and/or Proposal Committee by Wednesday, November 25, before the Week 13 instructor conversation. Reviewers should plan to return feedback around December 9.

The full sequence is on the Schedule page. Share the Advisor FAQ when you begin or confirm the mentoring relationship so the review window is not a surprise in November.

Part 3: Taking One Paper Apart Together

Everyone works from the same paper. I will lead the dissection at the board, and we will complete the Week 1 Literature Intake Form together. The form page includes a two-page printable PDF, and printed copies will be available. Keep the handout and your marked-up paper. They are the first entries in the claims-and-evidence record you will build across the course.

NoteReading for this session

Davis, A. S. (2010). Cover-crop roller-crimper contributes to weed management in no-till soybean. Weed Science, 58(3), 300-309. https://doi.org/10.1614/WS-D-09-00040.1

This is a guided structural pass, not a test and not yet a critical appraisal. We will work through four distinctions:

  1. What is the paper about?
  2. What question are the authors trying to answer?
  3. What is one central claim the authors make?
  4. What specific result, observation, figure, table, passage, or other evidence is connected to that claim?

We will record candidate answers, point to where they appear, and name what we are still uncertain about. “Candidate” matters. A first reading can produce a plausible interpretation that changes when we inspect the paper more closely.

The reading protocol gives you a starting procedure for empirical research articles. It is a heuristic, not a universal map. Different disciplines organize questions and evidence differently, and we will adapt the procedure as those differences appear.

Part 4: Your Work for the Rest of the Week

Interest, Motivation, and Direction Freewrite

Do not search for three faculty members yet. First, give yourself something worth searching from.

Write informally about:

  1. the problems, systems, questions, or experiences that hold your attention;
  2. why those interests matter to you;
  3. what motivates you to investigate or work on them;
  4. the kinds of activities or work you enjoy doing;
  5. a role, job, profession, or career that might involve that kind of work;
  6. what you already know and what you would need to explore; and
  7. one uncertainty you want help thinking through.

This is exploratory writing. You do not need a settled thesis topic, a research question, or polished prose. Spend enough time to produce material you can use in Monday’s topic-funnel exercise.

Submit the freewrite through Canvas before class on Monday, September 14. See Foundations Milestones for the satisfactory standard.

Fall Graduation Thesis Completion Track

Use the same prompts through the thesis you are already completing. Write about why the project mattered, how it connects to work or a future direction, what still motivates you, what you have learned about the work you want to do, and the most important uncertainty or threat to completion. We will address the formal thesis-status and deadline audit in the planning sequence that follows.

Before Next Week

  • Submit the Interest, Motivation, and Direction Freewrite before class Monday.
  • Bring a short sample of your academic or technical writing for the Week 2 writing diagnostic.
  • Keep the Week 1 handout and marked-up article. Bring them when we return to claim-and-evidence reading later in the semester.
