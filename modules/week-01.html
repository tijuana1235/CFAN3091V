Week 1: Taking a Paper Apart

Phase 1: Foundations

Learning Objectives

By the end of this week, you should be able to:

  • locate the research question, the central claim, and the supporting evidence in a research article;
  • name what each major section of a research article does for its reader;
  • describe the purpose of an honors thesis proposal;
  • identify a research domain and an initial set of research interests; and
  • identify three potential faculty mentors whose work connects to those interests.

One Meeting This Week

Labor Day falls on Monday, September 7, and instruction begins Tuesday, September 8. Our first and only meeting this week is Wednesday, September 9. We have 75 minutes. We need to understand how the course works, make the UHP and professional-review timeline visible, and begin learning how researchers read a paper. The paper clinic starts today and returns in Weeks 4, 6, and 8.

Why We Start With Reading

Almost everything you are graded on after Week 3 rests on one assumption: that you can open a research article and find the parts. Week 4 asks you to pull five sources out of a review article’s reference list. Week 6 asks you to decide whether a results section actually supports the sentence the authors wrote about it. Week 8 asks you to lay several papers side by side and say where they disagree.

Most undergraduate coursework never teaches that. You have been assigned readings and asked about their conclusions, which is a different activity from reading a paper the way a researcher reads one. That is a mechanical, learnable process, and the first pass does not take a semester to learn.

There is a second reason to start here. This course teaches technical writing, and the shortest route into technical writing runs through other people’s. You will draft an introduction in Week 10. Between now and then you will read many of them. Read them with the structure visible and you arrive at Week 10 with a model instead of a blank page.

You are not supposed to be good at this yet. That is what the semester is for.

Part 1: How This Course Runs

We work at the board and on paper. There are no slides and no deck to download afterward, so what happens in the room is the record. Bring paper and something to write with to every session.

You write every week. The proposal you finish in December is built out of fifteen weeks of smaller pieces, not out of one long December. Individual meetings replace class in Weeks 3, 7, and 13, and you come to those with a progress report in hand.

The full week-by-week sequence is on the Schedule page, and Canvas carries the most current version.

Most students follow the Proposal Development Track. Students graduating in Fall 2026 who have already filed the UHP Thesis Proposal Form and are actively completing the thesis follow the Fall Graduation Thesis Completion Track. We confirm your track this week.

The Primary Advisor and/or Proposal Committee must know early that an advisor-ready draft will arrive after your Week 13 instructor conversation and that feedback is needed around Dec 9. Share the Advisor FAQ when you begin or confirm that relationship. The review appointment is arranged before the draft exists.

Part 2: A First Dissection

Everyone works from the same paper, on paper, marked up by hand. I will take the first half apart at the board so you can see the moves. You will take the second half apart in pairs.

NoteReading for this session

Locate the article below through the University Libraries or another lawful source, and bring a printed copy to class:

Davis, A. S. (2010). Cover-crop roller-crimper contributes to weed management in no-till soybean. Weed Science, 58(3), 300-309. https://doi.org/10.1614/WS-D-09-00040.1

This is a structural pass, not a critical one. We are not asking yet whether the study is any good. We are asking what it says and where it says it. Four questions:

  1. What question were the authors trying to answer?
  2. What did they conclude? Find the sentence and underline it.
  3. What evidence is that conclusion resting on? Put your finger on the figure, the table, or the number.
  4. Where are the seams? Mark where each section begins and what job that section is doing.

Expect question 3 to stall the room. Conclusions are written to sound supported, and the distance between what a paper found and what its abstract says it found is often wider than you would guess. Finding that distance is a skill you will use for the rest of your career, and we keep sharpening it all term. Week 6 is an entire week of it.

The full procedure, including the order to read the sections in and what each section is doing, is on the reading protocol page. Bring it to every session where we read something.

Keep your marked-up copy. We come back to this paper later in the semester with harder questions.

Part 3: Your Work This Week

The domain and mentor work below normally happens in class. Labor Day takes that session, so it moves to your own time this term. It is not optional, and the Week 2 drills start from it.

Domain Map

  1. Name two or three problems, systems, or questions that hold your attention.
  2. Identify the disciplines that study each one.
  3. List what you already know and what you would need to investigate.
  4. Draft one tentative research-domain statement.
  5. Mark where your own statement is still too broad, too narrow, or ambiguous.

Bring this Monday. The topic funnel drill in Week 2 starts from what you write here, so a half-finished map costs you that session.

Mentor Search

  • Identify three potential mentors using departmental and faculty sources.
  • For each, record one specific paper, project, or course and what connects it to your interests.
  • Mark which of the three you understand well enough to ask a real question about, and which you only recognize by title.

Finding the right people is this week’s work. Writing to them is next week’s. You will draft those messages in Week 2, once you have a sharper problem to put in front of them.

Milestone

Submit the domain map and three potential mentors, each with the specific connection to your interests recorded, before the Week 2 Monday session on September 14. See Foundations Milestones for what counts as satisfactory.

Fall Graduation Thesis Completion Track

If you are graduating in Fall 2026, replace the domain and mentor map with a Thesis Status, Committee, and Deadline Audit. Record:

  • the status of each thesis section and remaining research or analysis;
  • the Primary Advisor and every confirmed or needed Proposal Committee member;
  • the June 30 proposal-form status;
  • the Dec 14 UHP completion deadline, the Dec 16 course-close submission, and the Dec 21 committee-approval deadline; and
  • the single most important threat to on-time completion.

Before Next Week

  • Bring a short sample of your academic or technical writing. You will use it to diagnose the writing habits you want to strengthen this semester.
  • Bring your marked-up copy of the Week 1 article and the reading protocol.
