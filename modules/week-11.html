Week 11: Expected Results and Discussion

Phase 3: Proposal Development

Week at a Glance

This week
Monday: project plausible outcomes and write the discussion those outcomes would require. Wednesday: review a partner’s methods and expected-results draft, then confirm the professional-review window.
Before class
Monday: bring your Week 10 draft, research question, methods, and literature synthesis. Fall Graduation students bring actual Results and Discussion pages instead of expected results.
Bring
Monday: the Two-Outcome Table worksheet. Wednesday: two printed copies of the relevant draft, the Week 11 Peer Review 2 worksheet, and the names and proposed dates for your Primary Advisor and/or Proposal Committee review.
Do
Monday: complete both outcome sketches and identify the evidence that would distinguish them. Wednesday: give and discuss Peer Review 2, then complete the Professional Review Plan before leaving class.
Due
Week 11: Peer Review 2 and Professional Review Plan in class on Wednesday, November 18.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this week, you should be able to:

  • distinguish expected results, which a proposal projects, from actual results, which a completed study reports;
  • write an expected results section that follows from your methods and literature rather than from what you hope to find;
  • draft a discussion connecting those expected findings to the literature and to their limitations; and
  • give substantive peer review on another writer’s methods and expected results.
ImportantKey Distinction

Previous semesters identified confusion between “expected results” (what you anticipate finding, based on logic and literature) and “actual results” (what you found, based on data). This module focuses exclusively on expected results: the kind you write for a proposal before data collection begins.

Monday: Writing Expected Results

Format: Short instruction, then drafting at the board and on paper

Proposal Development students write expected results. Fall Graduation students use the same logic to test whether their actual Results and Discussion stay within the evidence already collected.

Monday Plan

Topics

  • What “expected results” means in a proposal: projecting plausible outcomes, not making things up
  • The logic chain: hypothesis → prediction → expected outcome
  • What outcomes does your literature review suggest are likely?
  • What patterns do similar studies report?
  • Plausible alternatives: Expected result A if hypothesis holds; expected result B if it does not
  • Tables of anticipated findings: what would the data look like?
  • Writing the discussion: connecting findings to existing literature, acknowledging limitations, articulating significance, identifying future directions

Activity: The Two-Outcome Table

Most of Monday is drafting. Use the Two-Outcome Table worksheet, one row for the outcome if the hypothesis is supported and one for the outcome if it is not supported.

  1. Write your hypothesis in one sentence.
  2. Write the prediction that follows from it, in the units you would actually measure.
  3. Draw the table or figure you would expect if the hypothesis holds. Axes, units, and rough magnitudes. It does not need to be neat.
  4. Draw the second one: what the same table or figure looks like if the hypothesis does not hold.
  5. Write one sentence for each explaining what a reader should conclude.

Then trade with a partner and answer one question about their pair of sketches: could you tell these two outcomes apart if you saw the real data? If not, the prediction is not yet specific enough to be worth testing.

Discussion

  • For your study, what would it mean if your hypothesis is wrong? Is that a useful result?
  • How do you write about expected results without sounding like you are fabricating data?

Wednesday: Student Peer Review 2: Methods and Expected Results

Format: Peer review workshop, on paper

Proposal Development students exchange methods and expected results. Fall Graduation students exchange methods and actual Results and Discussion pages.

Activity

  1. Bring the methods section and outcomes pages from your Week 10: Introduction, Literature Review, and Methods Draft. Fall Graduation students bring the corresponding actual Results and Discussion pages.
  2. Exchange the relevant pages and use the published Week 11: Peer Review 2 and Professional Review Plan, plus the Peer Review 2 worksheet, which asks:
    • Could a reader identify the units, population or cases, materials, steps, measurements, and planned analysis without asking the writer to fill in the design?
    • Do the methods match the research question?
    • Are the expected results plausible given those methods?
    • Are alternative outcomes considered?
    • Is the discussion connected to the literature review?
  3. Provide written feedback with specific page or line references.
  4. Discuss the feedback face to face with your partner for about 10 minutes.
  5. Complete the Professional Review Plan, including the Nov 25 send-by date and expected feedback around Dec 9.

After Wednesday’s Review

Before the Week 12 submission, make the Peer Review 2 changes that most affect question-method-outcome alignment, feasibility, or the accuracy of your claims. You do not need to resolve every comment before submitting the working draft. Record comments you accept, modify, decline, or need to discuss in the Whole-Document Revision Memo you will complete in Week 12.

Method Soundness Check

Use the Week 6: Method and Feasibility Blueprint as the reference point. A method is inspectable when a reader can answer:

  • What units, population, system, cases, or materials are being studied?
  • What comparison or procedure will be used?
  • What outcome will be measured, and how?
  • What analysis or interpretive process will connect the evidence to the question?
  • What access, time, permission, ethics, or feasibility constraint could alter the plan?

If one answer is missing, mark the location and describe what the writer needs to specify. Do not rewrite the method for them.

Milestone Due

This week’s milestone has two parts, and both must be satisfactory for it to count:

  • Peer Review 2: structured worksheet completed and returned to your partner, with the expected results section drafted and brought to Wednesday so there is something to review. Hand the worksheet and writer response to the instructor on paper after the workshop.
  • Professional Review Plan: name the Primary Advisor and/or Proposal Committee, confirm that the released document will be sent no later than Nov 25, and record the expected feedback date around Dec 9. Confirm the review window with the people named in the plan. Hand the plan in on paper with the peer-review materials.

Fall Graduation Thesis Completion Track

Replace expected results with actual Results and Discussion work. Peer Review 2 should test whether the results are reported accurately, the discussion stays within the evidence, and limitations are visible. Reconfirm the same December professional-review window for the full thesis.

Before Next Week

  • Read the assigned materials on figures and tables listed on the course site.
  • Identify 2–3 figures or tables that would strengthen your proposal
  • Begin revising your proposal based on Peer Review 2 feedback
